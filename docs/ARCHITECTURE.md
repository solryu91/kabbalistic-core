# Architecture overview

## Design objective

The kernel makes its organizing structure observable. Kabbalistic names are not
labels pasted onto an ordinary response pipeline: graph nodes, worlds, paths,
permissions, state transitions, integration records, and stop reasons are typed
and testable. At the same time, no symbolic correspondence is treated as proof
of a technical or metaphysical claim.

## Main cycle

1. Keter binds one intention, active constraints, optional symbol, functional
   kernels, prior feedback, and evidence references.
2. Chokhmah and Binah open possibilities and give them typed form.
3. Chesed expands relevant context; Gevurah applies evidence, privacy,
   capability, and resource bounds.
4. Form, Flow, and Accord generate independent first-pass views before any one
   role can see another role's proposal.
5. Six directed observations record every ordered observer/peer pairing.
6. Tiferet scores candidate directions, records rejections, and selects one
   direction without allowing soft agreement to override a hard bound.
7. Netzach and Hod choose movement and formalize commitments.
8. Yesod seals a hash-bound context packet and proposed state projection.
9. Malkhut emits the outward response and records embodiment feedback.
10. Da'at can propose a durable promotion only through an explicit gate. A
    proposal is not a commit.

## Worlds and authority

Every transition belongs to one of four worlds: Atzilut (orientation), Beriah
(conceptual model), Yetzirah (relational form), or Assiah (embodied action).
Crossing into Assiah matters because it is where files, tools, interfaces, and
hardware could be affected. The permission layer keeps interpretation separate
from capability: recursion, retrieved text, model output, and consensus cannot
manufacture a grant.

## Functional kernels

`clear_sight`, `liberation`, and `regeneration` are project-owned configuration
records. They alter questions, salience, shadow checks, and target cores. They
are not personas, identities, agents, or independent authorities. Hidden
activation is off by default, and the activation record is visible in state.

## Retrieval

The retrieval layer tokenizes a query, scores a finite embedded corpus, binds
every selected excerpt to its content digest, and hashes the policy and ordered
selection. Retrieved blocks include an explicit statement that they are source
data, not instructions. The public server uses only the included synthetic
corpus.

## Neural and deterministic paths

The deterministic engine has no model, network, corpus, or external side
effect. The proof-of-concept service can ask a compatible loopback model for
bounded typed proposals. It validates schemas, evidence references, condition
bindings, call budgets, and preserved commitments. Failure produces an explicit
error or labeled deterministic fallback; it does not masquerade as a successful
neural graph run.

## Traces

Trace events record semantic transitions and resulting hashes. They are
inspectable process records, not hidden chain-of-thought. Completed traces are
validated against route, world, evidence, permission, integration, feedback,
and final-state invariants before export.

## Continuity

Continuity is a separate bounded service. It stores encrypted record payloads
in SQLite, keeps the master key external, and binds a logically append-only
record chain to a separately stored authority checkpoint. The released slice
implements one episode, one correction, exact consent binding, deterministic
reduction, independent reopen, and matched no-history comparisons. It is not
automatically connected to the main interface.
